Why it matters now

The same material, four different buyers

A phosphate investment might sell to a dental lab casting partial frames and to a jeweler casting resin-printed patterns. The chemistry is shared; the attributes, mixing instructions, dealers and compliance documents are not. Managing that by market in separate spreadsheets is how versions drift. Managing it as one record with market-specific views is how it stays right.

Technical documents

Mixing ratios, burnout schedules, SDS and TDS sheets have to be the current version at every dealer, in every market.

Dealer channel

Dealers list R&R products in their own catalogs and sites. They need clean, current content delivered to them, not found on a website.

Print-to-cast and new tech

Resin printing changes what customers ask about. New product lines need to reach every market fast.

The pattern we see

Where product content usually breaks down

We have not audited R&R's systems. What follows is the pattern we see at specialty materials manufacturers selling through dealers and the questions we would like to check against yours.

Content managed by market

  • Dental, jewelry and industrial teams each maintain their own spec sheets for shared chemistries
  • TDS and SDS documents are versioned by hand and emailed to dealers
  • The website, dealer catalogs and trade-show literature rarely say the same thing

Dealers do the re-keying

  • Each dealer builds its own listing from whatever R&R sent last
  • New products reach some dealers months after others
  • Nobody at R&R can see how a dealer is describing the product
